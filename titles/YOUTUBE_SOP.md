Packaging decides up to 80% of a video's performance, so the title gets chosen before scripting or filming, not after.

## Hard rules

- Under 70 characters or YouTube truncates it. 60–70 is the target.
- Put the target keyword in, in the words people actually search ("cash based practice", "deep work").
- One concept, one word. Never stack "best free tool".
- Numbers when they earn their place.
- Sell the transformation, not the video's information. Sell the end result.
- Build curiosity without giving away the payoff. Generate curiosity, don't chase agreement.
- The title has to match what the video delivers. No false promises.
- Produce 5–10 options, then shortlist the best 3 to A/B test.
- Style: Title Case or all lowercase, as a brand choice. Never all caps.

## Intent decides the title, not reach

- **TOF** — built to travel. Broad outcome, wider curiosity gap.
- **MOF** — a nurturing sales asset that builds trust. Speak to the qualified buyer; name the niche and the mechanism.
- A super-TOF title on a MOF video attracts the wrong, unqualified audience even when it wins more views. Going too niche kills the search. It's a balance.

## Proven formats (2026)

Adapt the structure, never the example.

- How to (outcome) so that (exaggerated consequence)
- I (mechanism), now I (desired outcome)
- (Thing) is hard, until you (do it) like this
- If I wanted to (outcome) [in 2026], I'd do this
- F*ck it, here's how (unique mechanism to outcome) — only where that's on brand

## Elements that lift a title

- Powerful, emotional adjectives, to stir interest.
- Numbers, to organise: "10 Ways To…", "7 Habits For…".
- Brackets or parentheses, to clarify or emphasise: (Full Breakdown), (2026 Update), (Full Course).
- Controversy, to provoke: the unspoken truth about X, why everyone is wrong about X.

## When stuck, fill these in

- How / who
- What they'll accomplish
- The objection that has to be handled
