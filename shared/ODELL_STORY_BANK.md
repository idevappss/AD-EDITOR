## 2. The story bank — in his own framing

### The walk-on
Wanted to play college basketball. Nobody D1 wanted him. Coach made a tape a week before signing day, Fleck offered a walk-on spot. Recruited as a D-end, moved to TE because he "was too slow." Walk-ons ate separately. Earned the scholarship in fall camp sophomore year.
His line: "I'm actually glad I was a walk-on because I earned my scholarship."
**Angle:** Earning it beats being handed it. Nobody picked him first — for basketball, for football, for PT school.

### The NFL removed his fear
Made it to a Browns locker room with Baker Mayfield and Njoku. Once he'd done the 0.01% thing, nothing in business scared him. "There's nothing that's going to be as hard as making the NFL."
PT school practicals: classmates cried before them; he'd "played in front of a hundred thousand people."
**Angle:** The hard thing you already did is the proof you can do the next one.

### Entrepreneur before doctor
"I'm an entrepreneur turned doctor of physical therapy." Uber after winning games. Scooter company with his own app — met with the mayor before COVID. DJ business from a scholarship check. Amazon, dropshipping. "Do it, do it, do it, keep learning fast."
**Angle:** Speed over perfection. "You only need one thing to work."

### The app that paid for school
Posted workouts. Fifteen people asked for programs. Collected a list, sold it, *then* built the app. $10K–$12K/month while a full-time DPT student.
His line: "My audience decided what they wanted."
**Angle:** Sell it, then build it. Let the audience tell you.

### The van
Not a hardship story. Car got totaled; rather than pay double rent while opening in Michigan, he bought a van, wrote it off, and reinvested. Five weeks solo in San Diego looking at beach houses: "Why not me?"
His line: "Everything happens for you, not to you."
**Angle:** Cut the expense, keep the vision. Freedom was the plan, not the consequence.

### Opening the clinic
Wrote a loose business plan so he wouldn't forget the original vision. Joined a mastermind *before* opening so he could "hit the ground running with speed." Saw patients with boxes on the floor. First month $33K. Paid years of rent in months.
**Angle:** Prepare before day one. Speed comes from preparation, not luck.

### Meeting Abby
She DM'd. He was heads-down and didn't answer for weeks. Mutual friend connected them on business. She owned two chiro offices, a restaurant, real estate. "We're very, very similar."
**Angle:** Find someone who can talk business "all day every day."

### Documenting, not creating
"I don't create content. I document my life." Filmed himself studying, walking into buildings, getting coffee four times a week. Has enough footage banked that he "can stop filming today and have content for seven months." Uses Google folders by patient/session so his editor can pull.
**Angle:** Your day is the content. Repetition is fine — 8 billion people haven't seen it.

### Anti-standardization
Raised his hand in PT school asking "why do we do it this way" and professors often didn't know. "Don't try to standardize me." Coaches talk to each player differently; school doesn't.
**Angle:** Question the system. Different people need different coaching.

### Money vs. mission
Framed publicly (True Sports 2026): clinicians are taught business is dirty, and that framing costs patients. A lean, ethical practice is the vehicle for impact, not the obstacle. Personal version (transcript): money is a tool to bring kingdom entrepreneurs closer to Christ; if it was about money he'd have quit long ago; "I serve an abundant God."
**Angle:** Being paid well and being a good Christian aren't in tension.

### Speaking / building leaders
"I used to hate speaking, then something switched." Now yearns for stages. Spoke ~10 times in two years by early 2024. True Sports 2026 theme: "building leaders beats managing people," "the implementation ladder."
**Angle:** Impact scales through people, not appointments.

### The mentor lens
Learned business "by osmosis" — Gary V on Uber drives, business podcasts on 4am workouts, reading how people he wanted to be like think. Virtual mentors count. Different mentors for different domains.
**Angle:** You don't need permission or proximity to learn from the best.

## New story material — in his own words

**The NFL plan was always business.** "Even when I was in the NFL, if my career lasted 10 years, I was going to start my own businesses." He frames not making a 10-year career as a question, not a failure: "Me not making an NFL for 10 years — is that a failure, or did I learn a lot of things that are going to take me well beyond where I ever thought I could?"

**The mission line.** Got "the highest level care at Division 1 and going to the NFL," saw in PT school that everyday people don't get it: "What I'm going to do is bring NFL and high-level care to everyday individuals."

**305 → 235.** Played lineman at about 305 lbs, got "shredded" to about 235 in PT school by posting his workouts. People asked for programs — that became the Off The Bench training app.

**The pricing lesson that built everything.** 12 people signed up at $80/month and weren't getting fast results. He got a coach, who told him to charge more. Moved to $500/month or $2,500–$3,000 packages — same program — and clients started getting results. "People pay for what they value." Clinic version: "I would rather see 15 patients a week at $500 each than 50 at $80. I'm going to get more no's, but I'm also going to get my time back."

**$20K/month before the clinic existed.** "Before I opened up the clinic, I was already making 20K a month online. So I had funds to open up my own spot from the start."

**The van, fully.** Got the van his last year of PT school after his car was totaled and his lease was ending: "Why don't I — I'm single — just get in the van, because I'm going to be working in coffee shops 24/7." Lived in it, parked behind the Off The Bench clinic or in a Planet Fitness lot, did laundry at a laundromat in Chicago. Woke at 4 a.m. — once got a knock from police telling him overnight parking had been banned for six months; he'd never noticed because he always left before 4. The clinic made enough in months one and two that he didn't need the van, and that money bought his **first rental property**. "I want to keep everything minimalist."

**4 a.m. for 3 years.** "I woke up at 4 a.m. for like 3 years — I had to, because I was in school and also running a business." He loves the quiet: "There's no one awake… I don't even have to check my phone for 5 hours."

**The scooter company paid off later.** "Everything that I learned with those scooters is what allowed me to get to a $51,000 month in my OTB clinic as a new grad within six months." Mentors later told him he couldn't build a multi-million-dollar business working in every sector at once. His father's line: "You have so many pans in the fire."

**Handing patients to Dr. Nick.** "I wouldn't put anyone in front of you who wouldn't deliver the level of care — or better — that I was giving you." New patients may come in off his NFL-athlete social proof, but Dr. Nick takes them from the consult.

**How he hires.** "I always tell my team: I'm not a manager. I'm a true entrepreneur." Asks about past, future goals, why here, long run; does a group interview with the whole team; has candidates shadow for a week — "I want to see how they talk to the receptionist." Favorite question for PT students: "Tell me how you were making money in grad school." Says he's "good at reading people."

**Don't name your clinic after yourself.** He named himself (the brand) coming out of the NFL for a podcast, but advises clinic owners against putting their name on the clinic.

**The Implementation Ladder (from Myron Golden).** Implementation → managing → communication → imagination. "I'm doing the world a disservice if I'm seeing all the patients." Helping 1,000 PTs open clinics reaches more patients than adding one more location. Communicating to a crowd "lights me up." Also reads *Buy Back Your Time* (Dan Martell).

**Faith, in his words.** "I believe in Jesus. I just want to be a light — to as many people as I can. Even in football or growing up, I wanted to be a light on the team." Ties it to independence: "I can't do everything everyone else is doing and expect a different result… it's just not following the crowd."

**Documenting.** "Even in PT school, I was showing myself studying in class." On vacation: "What did I do? I filmed what I was doing already."
